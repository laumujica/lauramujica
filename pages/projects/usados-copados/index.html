01 / Context

Useful objects get stuck. Trust gets in the way.

Usados Copados started from a familiar situation: I wanted to let go of things I no longer used, but many still had value. Existing marketplaces made it possible to publish them, yet they mixed new and used products and often gave too little context about the people on either side of an exchange.

I framed the opportunity as a marketplace dedicated to used goods in Argentina, with enough information to help people judge both a product and the person offering it. The native app remained a Figma prototype; later experiments explored the business idea through a different, no-code storefront.

Academic foundation3 months

Iterative UX/UI work reviewed through multiple deliveries by a professional designer, then approved as the final course project.

Business explorationLater course

I returned to the idea through a Business Model Canvas, without turning that exercise into a new digital product.

Operational experiment1 year

I maintained an Empretienda storefront under usadoscopados.ar. This tested the marketplace idea, not the Figma app itself.

02 / Research

I used research to turn a broad idea into a product structure.

I compared Mercado Libre, OLX, Wallapop, and Facebook Marketplace, looking at navigation, product discovery, profile information, reputation, and the role each platform played in a transaction.

The comparison exposed a useful gap: a focused second-hand experience could make trust cues more prominent instead of treating them as secondary information. I translated that direction into scenarios and two provisional personas—a seller and a buyer—to keep the early flows concrete.

03 / Information architecture

Ten participants helped reorganize the mental model.

I ran a hybrid card sort with 10 participants between 20 and 65 years old. They grouped 20 content cards into suggested categories and could create new categories when the model did not fit.

The similarity matrix and dendrogram showed strong agreement around account-related content and the relationship between product information and promotion. They also exposed unclear labels—notably “Buyer” and “Seller”—and no meaningful agreement about Notifications.

Three IA iterations, not one polished answer

I first adapted the hypothetical architecture to the card-sort results, then revisited the difficult cards through informal follow-ups, and finally used a Crazy 8 exercise with classmates and a tutor to explore a different publishing model.

Resulting primary navigationHome · History · Publish · My Account

Messaging remained available in context rather than occupying a primary destination. Publish became the point where different exchange intentions were made explicit.

04 / Core flow

The MVP connected publishing, discovery, and trust.

The main Figma flow followed one scenario: publish a camera, find another model within a budget, and inspect the seller before deciding whether to make contact. This linked the marketplace action to the trust information that supported it.

  1. PublishChoose Sell, add details and photographs, review, publish.
  2. SearchEnter a query, scan results, use filters and sorting.
  3. EvaluateRead the listing, open the seller profile, compare history and ratings.
Low-fidelity wireflow

Connected before it was polished

The early prototype covered the entire task path rather than isolated screens. That made it possible to test labels, sequence, scrolling, search behavior, photo upload, and profile navigation together.

05 / Usability testing

Five moderated sessions exposed what visual polish had hidden.

5participants
5task groups
21minutes average

I moderated remote sessions using Figma and Meet with adults who had bought or sold used products online. I reviewed the recordings again as an observer to capture behavior I could not record while moderating.

The sample did not include the older adult and participant with a disability I had intended to recruit, so I did not treat these sessions as accessibility validation.

Observed frictionDesign response
Sell vs. Offer created hesitation because the distinction was not self-evident.Clarify the two models during onboarding and at the publishing decision.
Small, low-contrast text made confirmation and product information difficult to read.Increase type sizes, strengthen text colors, and reduce content density.
Photo upload used imagery and an icon that some participants misread.Use a more familiar upload pattern with explicit guidance and feedback.
Navigation state was weak: history was absent and selected tabs were unclear.Add persistent navigation and visible active/selected states.
Seller status and ratings needed more context to support judgment.Clarify online status, reduce unrealistic counts, and expand profile evidence.

Professional feedback loop

Three months of staged reviews

Each delivery received observations from a UX/UI design professional. I used that feedback to revisit the structure, task flow, testing documentation, and interface before the project received final approval.

07 / UI system

The visual identity became a reusable interface language.

I translated the moodboard into documented foundations in Figma: a Roboto Flex type scale, named color roles, icon and avatar families, an 8 px grid, a four-column mobile structure, and spacing based on multiples of eight.

The file predates Figma Variables, so its token layer lives in named local paint and text styles. Components, variants, and atomic naming then carry those decisions into navigation, cards, buttons, search, forms, feedback, and profile patterns.

Typography16 text styles

Title, label, body, headline, subheadline, and button roles built with Roboto Flex.

Color23 paint styles

Primary, secondary, neutral, success, warning, error, and information families.

Layout8 px foundation

Four mobile columns with 16 px margins and gutters, plus a spacing scale based on eight.

ArchitectureAtomic structure

Atoms, molecules, and organisms named to expose composition and reuse.

Component coverage

Top and bottom navigation · product cards · buttons · search bars · chips · segmented controls · forms · photo galleries · dialogs · toasts · ratings · status and notification states.

System boundary

This is a project-level Figma system, not a production library with code parity, governance, or releases. Its value is the repeatable logic used to build and revise a large connected prototype.

Reflection

What the project proved—and what it did not.

Usados Copados gave me a complete design loop: start from a personal observation, investigate the market, model the information, build a testable flow, receive professional feedback, watch people use it, and let those observations change the interface.

The Figma prototype showed that its core tasks could be understood by the five people I tested with, while revealing issues around language, legibility, and state. The later Business Model Canvas and year-long Empretienda experiment explored the idea from different angles; they did not validate the native app, environmental impact, or accessibility at scale.

If I continued today, I would retest the revised Sell/Offer explanation, include older and disabled participants, define rating criteria more carefully, and migrate the style-based token layer into Figma Variables with clearer component documentation.
